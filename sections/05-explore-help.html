10 · Hamarøy

Explore the area

Hamarøy is a place for coast, mountains, art, literature and weather. Opening hours and conditions change with the season, so check current information before setting out.

Tranøy & Tranøy Lighthouse

Tranøy is a small coastal village known for its dramatic landscape, art, historic trading traditions and wide views across Vestfjorden. Tranøy Lighthouse, dating from 1864 and still operational, is reached by a roughly 250-metre pedestrian bridge across the rocks.

Depending on season and opening hours, you may be able to walk out to the lighthouse, join a guided lighthouse tour, eat at the lighthouse restaurant, explore the coastal landscape, visit galleries and outdoor sculptures, or simply enjoy the midnight sun or northern lights.

Take care: the rocks and bridge approaches can be slippery in rain or frost, and high waves can make the shoreline dangerous. Keep children under close supervision.

Hamarøyskaftet & Skaftheia

Hamarøyskaftet is the distinctive, pillar-shaped mountain visible from much of outer Hamarøy. The hike towards Skaftheia, approximately 400 metres above sea level, offers excellent views of Hamarøyskaftet, the surrounding mountains and Vestfjorden.

The normal walking route leads towards Skaftheia and the lower part of Hamarøyskaftet. The summit of Hamarøyskaftet is a technical climbing route and must not be treated as an ordinary hike. The final section requires climbing skills, suitable equipment and preferably a qualified local guide.

The starting point is signposted from the main road near Halsen along County Road 17. A toll fee is required to drive all the way to the parking area, where the marked walking route begins. Wear proper hiking footwear and avoid the route in poor visibility, strong wind, ice or heavy rain.

Glimma

A scenic walking area near Presteid and the Hamsun Centre, combining coastal scenery, cultural history, tidal water and mountain views. A good option when you want a less demanding outing than a mountain hike.

The Hamsun Centre & childhood home

The Hamsun Centre at Presteid is both an architectural landmark and a museum about Knut Hamsun's life, writing and controversial legacy. His childhood home in Hamsund is a separate museum with seasonal opening.

Art in Tranøy

Tranøy has a long-standing artistic community with galleries, workshops and outdoor sculptures. Cafés, galleries and exhibitions can have limited or seasonal opening hours.

Fishing

Vestfjorden and the waters around Hamarøy offer excellent sea fishing. Depending on season and location, common species can include cod, coalfish, pollock, mackerel, haddock and halibut. Options include fishing from suitable shoreline locations, fishing around Tranøy where access is permitted, joining a guided trip, or fishing from a properly equipped boat.

Sea fishing with handheld tackle is generally free, but national regulations, minimum sizes, protected species and local restrictions must be followed. Keep at least 100 metres away from fish farms and never fish close to aquaculture installations, moorings or working vessels.

Freshwater fishing normally requires permission or a local fishing licence. Additional rules apply for salmon, sea trout and Arctic char. Fishing equipment may need to be cleaned and disinfected before being used in a different river or watercourse. Sea conditions can change quickly — use a life jacket when boating and always check the marine forecast.

Beaches & coastal walks

Outer Hamarøy has attractive beaches, small coves and rocky coastal areas. The water is cold even in summer, and waves, wind, currents and slippery rocks can make the coast hazardous. Children must always be supervised near the sea.

Northern lights

From autumn through early spring, Brennvik Panorama can offer excellent conditions for seeing the northern lights. Choose a clear, dark evening, switch off unnecessary outdoor and indoor lights and give your eyes time to adjust. Look towards the north, but remember that strong displays may appear across the entire sky.

Dress warmly and stay clear of roads, cliffs and slippery shorelines. Northern-light activity can never be guaranteed, even when the forecast is favourable.

Midnight sun & light summer nights

Early summer brings exceptionally bright nights. Tranøy, the lighthouse and west-facing coastal viewpoints are beautiful places to experience the evening light. Even in summer, temperatures by the sea can drop quickly — bring an extra layer.

Hellmobotn

Hellmobotn lies deep inside the dramatic Tysfjord landscape and is reached by scheduled boat services from locations including Drag during parts of the summer. From Hellmobotn, marked hiking routes continue towards the Swedish border through remote mountain terrain.

This is a full-day or multi-day wilderness experience. Boat timetables, weather, route conditions, daylight and your own experience must be checked carefully in advance.

Cycling & winter activities

The quieter roads around Hamarøy are well suited to scenic cycling, including routes towards Tranøy and around outer Hamarøy. Roads can be narrow and some sections have loose gravel. Wear a helmet, use lights or reflective clothing and remain alert for local traffic.

Depending on snow and weather, winter activities can include snowshoeing, cross-country skiing, winter photography, northern-light viewing and short coastal walks. Avoid exposed mountains and shorelines during storms, poor visibility or avalanche conditions. Daylight is limited in midwinter, so carry a headlamp and suitable emergency clothing.

Groceries

Tranøy Landhandel is the local grocery option in Tranøy, approximately 10–15 minutes away by car. It operates with self-service access for extended hours; the exact access and payment arrangement can change, so check current information before relying on late-night access.

SPAR Hamarøy at Oppeid is a larger supermarket, also roughly 10–15 minutes away by car. Opening hours can change during holidays and outside the main season.

Safety in nature

Weather in Northern Norway can change rapidly. Before hiking, fishing or boating: check the current weather and marine forecast, tell someone where you are going, carry a charged mobile phone, wear suitable footwear and clothing, and bring water, food and an extra warm layer.

Turn back before conditions become unsafe. Do not rely exclusively on mobile coverage. Keep a safe distance from waves, cliffs and exposed edges, leave no rubbish, and respect wildlife, private property and local residents.
